AI policies for instructors

US universities and their computer science courses. AI Policy Atlas, November 18, 2026. Website: https://rudrajit1729.github.io/ai-policy-atlas/#instructors. Sources accessed and links checked: November 18, 2026.

Who this is for. These are the AI policies that universities, schools and computing departments write for their instructors: what exists, and what instructors are asked to do. They come from provosts, teaching centers, academic integrity offices and department committees, so the first readers are the administrators and committees who write such policies and the instructors who receive them. The CS course level adds the few policies instructors write for themselves in their own syllabi.

About this document

This document lists every policy that US university documents and CS course syllabi address to instructors, drawn from 774 university-wide, school and department documents at 342 universities and from AI passages in CS course syllabi.

A policy is one thing asked of one group. Statements that ask instructors to do the same thing are merged into one policy, whatever their wording, and each policy is written as a directive to the reader. Within each level, policies are grouped by stance (what the policy does about AI use), then follow the course from before it starts to after it ends, then are grouped by what they ask. The groups come from a thematic analysis by three independent coders. Policies that make the same point appear as one entry led by the most widespread policy, with the others listed as variants and how they differ.

Each entry is tagged must/should you are asked to do it (a rule or a recommendation), may you may do it, or need not: the choice is yours, or fact a fact or warning that asks nothing of you. It also gives the number of universities and of statements behind it (“13 (15)” means 15 statements from 13 different universities), the levels where it appears (U university-wide, S school or college, C computing department), and its ID. Sources: every ID links to the website, which lists each statement behind the policy in the institution’s own words, with a link to the source.

Policies addressed to everyone in a university also apply to instructors; the website lists them on its Other roles page.

University, school and department level: policies for instructors

The institutional documents yield 249 policies that address instructors, merged from 5,525 statements at 303 universities. Policies cluster early in the course: 96 apply before it and 55 at its start, against 43 during the course, 11 during assessment and 10 over time; another 28 address suspected misuse and 6 give background facts. Over half of the policies, 132 of 249, take no position on AI and instead set cross-cutting duties, such as including an AI statement in the syllabus. Of the rest, 43 allow AI only for listed uses, 34 encourage it, 20 allow it with conditions, 8 leave the decision to the instructor, 6 give facts and warnings as context, and 3 each prohibit AI or require something when it is used. Although only 8 policies leave the decision to the instructor, 252 of the 303 universities state at least one of them, and only the cross-cutting duties, at 280 universities, reach more.

University, school and department level · Prohibit: 3 policies, 8 universities

AI is not allowed. Two of the 3 prohibiting policies apply before the course, and both concern students' work: instructors make clear that only original, personal work counts for assessment (P29.01, 6 universities), and 1 university asks instructors in writing-intensive courses to assign writing that students complete without generative AI (P01.02). The third, also from 1 university, forbids instructors from presenting AI-generated content as their own original work (P74.05).

University, school and department level · Only listed uses: 43 policies, 175 universities

AI is allowed only for listed uses. The 43 policies that allow AI only for listed uses appear at every stage of the course except Over time, and the most widespread of them concern detection tools. When misuse is suspected, instructors should not treat a detector result as the only evidence of misconduct (P69.02, 63 universities), and 58 universities tell instructors not to run student work through detectors at all (P39.03). Others concern student data and approved tools: during the course, instructors keep student work and identifiable information out of AI tools unless the student consents or the tool is approved (P50.08, 39 universities), and before the course they choose only approved or university-licensed tools (P92.01, 27 universities). At assessment, instructors keep a human accountable for AI-assisted grades (P46.09, 18 universities) or do not use AI to grade at all (P46.03, 17 universities), and 13 universities ask them to move exams to in-person, supervised formats (P33.04).

University, school and department level · Allowed with conditions: 20 policies, 85 universities

AI is allowed under conditions such as disclosure. The most widespread policies that allow AI with conditions concern instructors' own use of it. Before the course, instructors may use AI to draft and refine syllabi, rubrics, quiz questions and other course materials (P59.01), a policy stated by 50 universities in 114 statements. During the course, instructors may use approved tools to draft feedback or score work against a rubric (P59.05, 16 universities), and 6 universities add that instructors must personalize AI-drafted feedback (P101.05); when misuse is suspected, they may use detector results only as preliminary or supporting evidence (P69.05, 17 universities). Other policies let instructors allow students to use AI for listed purposes, such as study aids and practice exams (P14.01, 7 universities) or editing and proofreading (P91.01, 6 universities).

University, school and department level · Encourage: 34 policies, 171 universities

AI use is invited or promoted. Of the 34 policies that encourage AI, 17 apply during the course, and none applies during assessment. Before the course, instructors are asked to design assignments that build in student AI use (P79.01), stated by 64 universities, more than any other encouraging policy, and to run their own assignments through AI tools to see how AI handles them (P63.04, 37 universities). During the course, instructors teach students how AI works and what it can and cannot do (P44.04, 45 universities), teach them to use it ethically (P44.06, 35 universities), and assign them to critique or verify AI output (P31.08, 28 universities). Only 3 encouraging policies apply over time, each stated by 3 or fewer universities, such as sharing AI assignments and syllabus statements with colleagues (P63.16, 3 universities).

University, school and department level · Instructor decides: 8 policies, 252 universities

The instructor or assignment decides. Although only 8 policies leave the AI decision to the instructor, they include the most widespread of all 249 policies. At the start of the course, instructors tell students clearly whether and how they may use AI (P09.03), a policy stated by 193 universities in 389 statements. Before the course, instructors write a course AI policy (P04.02, 82 universities), which they may adapt from sample syllabus statements (P110.02, 72 universities), and during the course they state AI expectations for each assignment (P09.09, 85 universities). The only such policy for suspected misuse leaves the consequences to the instructor's discretion (P37.08, 16 universities).

University, school and department level · Cross-cutting duties: 132 policies, 280 universities

What you must or should do whatever the stance on AI use. The 132 cross-cutting policies set duties for instructors, and 56 of them apply before the course. The most widespread of those concern assessment design: instructors base AI decisions on the course's learning objectives (P83.01, 71 universities), redesign assignments and assessments in response to generative AI (P35.01, 58 universities), and assess students' learning process along with the final product (P35.02, 49 universities). The 39 policies at the start of the course concern telling students the rules, led by including a generative AI statement in the syllabus (P09.02, 191 universities, 323 statements) and explaining how to cite or acknowledge AI use (P24.03, 83 universities); 62 universities also ask instructors to tell students whether and how they use AI themselves (P47.01). The 21 policies for suspected misuse ask instructors to talk with the student in a supportive way before escalating (P57.01, 66 universities) and to report suspected misuse promptly to the academic integrity office (P81.03, 57 universities), and 47 universities say instructors may use detection or plagiarism tools (P40.07). Few apply later in the course: 8 during the course, 1 during assessment and 7 over time. The most widespread of these ask instructors to check AI output they produce and remain responsible for it (P31.05, 44 universities) and to remind students of the policy through the semester (P66.13, 35 universities).
