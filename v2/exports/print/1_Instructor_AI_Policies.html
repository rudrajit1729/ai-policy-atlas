AI policies for instructors

US universities and their computer science courses. AI Policy Atlas, November 18, 2026. Website: https://rudrajit1729.github.io/ai-policy-atlas/#instructors. Sources accessed and links checked: November 18, 2026.

Who this is for. These are the AI policies that universities, schools and computing departments write for their instructors: what exists, and what instructors are asked to do. They come from provosts, teaching centers, academic integrity offices and department committees, so the first readers are the administrators and committees who write such policies and the instructors who receive them. The CS course level adds the few policies instructors write for themselves in their own syllabi.

About this document

This document lists every policy that US university documents and CS course syllabi address to instructors, drawn from 774 university-wide, school and department documents at 342 universities and from AI passages in CS course syllabi.

A policy is one thing asked of one group. Statements that ask instructors to do the same thing are merged into one policy, whatever their wording, and each policy is written as a directive to the reader. Within each level, policies are grouped by stance (what the policy does about AI use), then follow the course from before it starts to after it ends, then are grouped by what they ask. The groups come from a thematic analysis by three independent coders. Policies that make the same point appear as one entry led by the most widespread policy, with the others listed as variants and how they differ.

Each entry is tagged Expected (instructors are expected to do it: the policy says they should or must), Choice (instructors may do it, or not: the choice is theirs) or Fact (a fact or warning that asks nothing of anyone). It also gives the number of universities and of statements behind it (“13 (15)” means 15 statements from 13 different universities), the levels where it appears (U university-wide, S school or college, C computing department), and its ID. Sources: every ID links to the website, which lists each statement behind the policy in the institution’s own words, with a link to the source.

Policies addressed to everyone in a university also apply to instructors; the website lists them on its Other roles page.

University, school and department level: policies for instructors

The institutional documents yield 249 policies that address instructors, merged from 5,525 statements at 303 universities. Policies cluster early in the course: 96 apply before it and 56 at its start, against 43 during the course, 11 during assessment and 8 over time; another 29 address suspected misuse and 6 give background. Over half of the policies, 145 of 249, take no position on AI and instead set cross-cutting duties, such as including an AI statement in the syllabus. Of the rest, 36 allow AI only for listed uses, 30 encourage it, 21 allow it with conditions, 7 leave the decision to the instructor, 5 give facts and warnings as context, 3 require something when AI is used and 2 prohibit it. The cross-cutting duties are also the most widely stated, at 296 of the 303 universities, followed by the policies that allow AI only for listed uses (167 universities) and those that encourage it (165 universities). Of the 249, 199 ask instructors to do something, 45 leave the choice to them and 5 state facts or warnings.

University, school and department level · Only listed uses: 36 policies, 167 universities

AI is allowed only for listed uses. The 36 policies that allow AI only for listed uses appear at every stage except Over time, and the most widespread of them concern detection tools. When misuse is suspected, instructors should not treat a detector result as the only evidence of misconduct (P69.02, 63 universities), and 58 universities tell instructors not to run student work through detectors at all (P39.03). Others concern student data and approved tools: during the course, instructors keep student work and identifiable information out of AI tools unless the student consents or the tool is approved (P50.08, 39 universities), and before the course they choose only approved or university-licensed tools (P92.01, 27 universities). At assessment, instructors keep a human accountable for AI-assisted grades (P46.09, 18 universities) or do not use AI to grade at all (P46.03, 17 universities). Before the course, 13 universities also ask them to move exams and major assessments to in-person, supervised formats (P33.04).

University, school and department level · Allowed with conditions: 21 policies, 86 universities

AI is allowed under conditions such as disclosure. The most widespread policies that allow AI with conditions concern instructors' own use of it. Before the course, instructors may use AI to draft and refine syllabi, rubrics, quiz questions and other course materials (P59.01), a policy stated by 50 universities in 114 statements. During the course, instructors may use approved tools to draft feedback or score work against a rubric (P59.05, 16 universities), and 6 universities let them use AI to help draft formative feedback, flag patterns in student responses and generate ideas for feedback (P101.05); when misuse is suspected, they may use detector results only as preliminary or supporting evidence (P69.05, 17 universities). Other policies let instructors allow students to use AI for listed purposes, such as study aids and practice exams (P14.01, 7 universities) or editing and proofreading (P91.01, 6 universities).

University, school and department level · Encourage: 30 policies, 165 universities

AI use is invited or promoted. Of the 30 policies that encourage AI, 16 apply during the course, and none applies during assessment. Before the course, instructors are asked to design assignments that build in student AI use (P79.01), stated by 64 universities, more than any other encouraging policy, and to run their own assignments through AI tools to see how AI handles them (P63.04, 37 universities). During the course, instructors teach students how AI works and what it can and cannot do (P44.04, 45 universities), teach them to use it ethically (P44.06, 35 universities), and assign them to critique or verify AI output (P31.08, 28 universities). Only 1 encouraging policy applies over time; it encourages instructors to seek grants and support for AI work (P122.10, 1 university).

CS course syllabi say little about instructors. Across 19 universities, they yield 13 policies that address instructors, merged from 30 statements, compared with 249 policies from 5,525 statements in the institutional documents. The largest group, 5 of the 13, gives background facts, no other stage has more than 2 policies, and no course policy applies over time. By stance, 5 of the 13 are facts and warnings that give context, 2 leave the decision to the instructor, 2 require something, and 1 each prohibits AI, allows it only for listed uses, encourages it or falls under Cross-cutting duties; none allows AI with conditions. Some are commitments the syllabus makes about the instructor's own conduct, such as never using AI to give students feedback on their assessments. Of the 13, 4 ask instructors to do something, 4 leave the choice to them and 5 state facts or warnings.
