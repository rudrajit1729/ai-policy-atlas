AI policies for students

US universities and their computer science courses. AI Policy Atlas, November 18, 2026. Website: https://rudrajit1729.github.io/ai-policy-atlas/#students. Sources accessed and links checked: November 18, 2026.

Who this is for. These are the AI policies written for students. At the university, school and department level they come from administrators, academic integrity offices and departments. At the CS course level they come from instructors, in their syllabi: instructors writing for their students. The first readers are the instructors and administrators who write such policies and the students who must follow them.

About this document

This document lists every policy that US university documents and CS course syllabi address to students, drawn from 774 university-wide, school and department documents at 342 universities and from AI passages in CS course syllabi.

A policy is one thing asked of one group. Statements that ask students to do the same thing are merged into one policy, whatever their wording, and each policy is written as a directive to the reader. Within each level, policies are grouped by stance (what the policy does about AI use), then follow the course from before it starts to after it ends, then are grouped by what they ask. The groups come from a thematic analysis by three independent coders. Policies that make the same point appear as one entry led by the most widespread policy, with the others listed as variants and how they differ.

Each entry is tagged Expected (students are expected to do it: the policy says they should or must), Optional (students may do it, or not: the choice is theirs) or Fact (a fact or warning that asks nothing of anyone). It also gives the number of universities and of statements behind it (“13 (15)” means 15 statements from 13 different universities), the levels where it appears (U university-wide, S school or college, C computing department), and its ID. Sources: every ID links to the website, which lists each statement behind the policy in the institution’s own words, with a link to the source.

Policies addressed to everyone in a university also apply to students; the website lists them on its Other roles page.

University, school and department level: policies for students

The 130 university, school and department policies for students merge 2,841 statements from 301 universities, and 93 of the 130 apply during the course. The other stages hold few policies, with 2 before the course, 6 at its start, 3 during assessment, 9 when misuse is suspected and 2 over time; another 15 give background, 13 of them facts and warnings. By stance, Allowed with conditions is the largest group with 32 policies, followed by Only listed uses with 29 and Cross-cutting duties with 26, and 20 policies give facts and warnings as context. Only 9 policies prohibit AI, 9 encourage it, 4 require something when AI is used, and 1 leaves the decision to the instructor. Of the 130, 93 ask students to do something, 17 leave the choice to them and 20 state facts or warnings.

University, school and department level · Prohibit: 9 policies, 119 universities

AI is not allowed. The 9 prohibitions are stated by 119 universities, and 7 of them apply during the course. The most widespread forbids students to present AI-generated work as their own (P19.04), a rule stated by 94 universities in 132 statements. The others name particular dishonest uses, such as fabricating information or concealing AI use (P36.03, 16 universities), fabricating data, citations or evidence (P74.02, 12 universities) and forging records such as medical excuses (P74.15, 3 universities). One prohibition applies during assessment and treats unauthorized notes, devices or outside resources in an exam as cheating (P22.03, 9 universities), and no prohibition applies before the course, at its start, when misuse is suspected or over time.

University, school and department level · Only listed uses: 29 policies, 180 universities

AI is allowed only for listed uses. The 29 Only listed uses policies, stated by 180 universities, limit how students use AI as they work (25 of the 29) and what they may put into AI tools. The 2 most widespread of these 25 ask students to use AI to support their learning without using it as a shortcut or replacement (P32.03, 71 universities) and to keep personal, confidential or sensitive information out of AI tools (P50.06, 59 universities). Others ask students to understand everything they submit (P30.02, 24 universities), to use university-approved tools signed in with their university account (P92.06, 23 universities), and to keep course materials out of AI tools unless the instructor permits it (P49.04, 18 universities). The only such policy for when misuse is suspected, stated by 70 universities, says that students may not submit AI-generated content without acknowledging it (P36.09), and the only policy before the course asks students to get advisor or committee approval before using AI in theses or research (P62.03, 13 universities).

University, school and department level · Allowed with conditions: 32 policies, 146 universities

AI is allowed under conditions such as disclosure. Of the 32 Allowed with conditions policies, stated by 146 universities, 29 apply during the course, and the most widespread make disclosure the condition. Researchers disclose and cite AI in research outputs, theses and proposals (P62.06, 46 universities), and students follow their instructor's requirements for citing AI (P24.08, 27 universities) or cite it in a style such as APA or MLA (P25.07, 24 universities). A second group allows AI as a study aid, for generating practice questions and flashcards (P14.02, 36 universities), as a tutor or role-play partner (P14.04, 12 universities) and for exam preparation (P14.05, 11 universities), while some uses need the instructor's permission, such as summarizing readings (P105.06, 6 universities) and brainstorming (P15.07, 5 universities). The only policy at the start of the course lets students use AI when the instructor gives no AI statement, provided they disclose all use (P06.05, 1 university).

University, school and department level · Cross-cutting duties: 26 policies, 244 universities

What you must or should do whatever the stance on AI use. The 26 Cross-cutting duties policies are stated by 244 universities and include the most widely stated policy in this part. Students are told to ask their instructor when unsure whether AI use is allowed (P10.04, 193 universities, 386 statements) and to follow the university's academic integrity policy, honor code and conduct rules when using AI (P77.11, 56 universities). Of the 9 policies for when misuse is suspected, 3 are cross-cutting duties; they describe appeals (P64.02, 7 universities) and ask students to report unethical AI use or AI errors to their instructor (P71.08, 2 universities). At the start of the course, 57 universities ask students to read the syllabus and assignment instructions to learn what AI use is allowed (P78.02).

CS course level · Require: 39 policies, 83 universities

Something is required when AI is used (or AI use is required). Of the 39 Require policies, stated by 83 universities, 26 set what students must disclose or record when they use AI. The most widespread asks students to disclose or cite any AI use, permitted use included (CT25.c01, 40 universities), and others name the record, such as the prompts (CT27.c01, 14 universities), the full conversation transcript (CT27.c02, 8 universities) or the exact lines AI generated (CT26.c02, 7 universities). A second group makes students accountable for what they submit; they must be able to explain all code they submit (CT30.c02, 31 universities) and must verify AI output before using it (CT31.c01, 24 universities). A few policies require AI use itself, such as projects built around AI coding agents (CT21.c01, 2 universities) and a paid Claude Pro subscription (CT46.c06, 1 university).
