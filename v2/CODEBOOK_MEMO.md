# Codebook memo (constant-comparison log)

One entry per codebook decision: date, what changed (new, merged, split or redefined type), the units that prompted it, and why.

## 2026-10-25: Codebook closed after the dry run (v1 → closed list P01–P65)

Sources: proposals from Coder A and Coder B in the 6-university dry run, decided by the reconcilers and reviewed by Claude.

- **P58 Students write their own course communication** added (accountability). Messages, emails or posts that students send to course staff must be written by the student, not generated by AI (source: UC Irvine ICS 33/H32).
- **P59 Instructor use of AI for teaching work** added (faculty). What instructors or staff may, should or should not use AI for in their own teaching work: drafting syllabi, rubrics, quiz items, examples, feedback drafts, translations, letters. Final grading decisions are P46; disclosure to students is P47; AI-made course materials are P48 (sources: UIUC CITL and Grainger; Oregon State EECS).
- **P60 Decline to police AI use** added (enforcement). Staff state they will not actively police or detect AI use, relying instead on unassisted assessments or student responsibility (source: UIUC ECE 220).
- **P61 Define AI and the policy's scope** added (governance). Definitions of AI, generative AI, AI tools or agentic systems that set what a policy covers (sources: Oregon State, Howard).
- **P62 Govern AI use in research and scholarship** added (governance). AI rules for theses, dissertations, manuscripts, grants and peer review, including deferring to venue, funder, sponsor or IRB rules (sources: Oregon State EECS, Howard Policy 100-022).
- **P63 Support faculty in learning AI** added (faculty). Workshops, consultations, faculty communities, and advice that instructors try AI tools and learn their implications before setting course policy (sources: Oregon State, Howard).
- **P64 Students may respond to AI-misuse allegations** added (enforcement). Students accused of AI misuse have a right to respond or present evidence; fairness and transparency in AI-related integrity cases (source: Howard).
- **P65 Review AI vendor agreements and data sharing** added (responsible). Institutional review of AI contracts and partnerships, and limits on licensing or sharing university data and IP with AI providers (source: Howard Policy 100-022).
- `Prescribed AI interaction protocol` merged into **P35** (an assignment structured around AI; one occurrence; Coder B had already coded it P35).
- `Instructor AI for feedback and grading` folded into **P59**: final grade decisions remain P46.
- `Instructor AI familiarity before policy` folded into **P63**.
- `Defer to external venue and sponsor rules` folded into **P62**.

## 2026-10-26: review 2, after batch 3 (174 universities; D38, D39)
I read all 124 new UNCOVERED notes and all 1,027 fit notes from batch 3, which is 29% of its 3,576 units.

**Added 26 types (P78–P103), each seen at 3 or more universities; evidence is in each type's `covers` field:** students learn and follow each course's rules; require AI use in some coursework; who owns, enforces and grants exceptions; formal integrity-reporting procedure; AI use cannot be fully prevented or verified; align AI with learning objectives; how the policy was made; schools or units set their own rules; consent before AI recording; benefits of AI for learning; engage peers and community; AI for data analysis; own work first, then AI; AI for editing and writing support; university-licensed tools; prompting advice; careers as a reason to build skills without AI; fairness and honesty as the reason; AI is not an author, source or collaborator; accessibility; documenting and auditing AI systems; reducing the pressures that lead to misuse; exempting routine AI features; conditions for AI-assisted grading; staying informed; telling people when they are interacting with AI.

**Broadened two types:** P65 (any AI tool review) and P69 (detectors only as supporting evidence).

**What most fit notes were really about** (D38). These are not new content; the theme review will fix them:
1. Type names that build in modality or actor. Examples: "P09 says *require*, but this unit only recommends"; "P24 is addressed to instructors here, not students". The fix is content-only type names, with modality and actor carried by their own tags.
2. "Not AI-specific", about 60 notes: general integrity rules applied to AI by extension. The fix is a new `ai_specific` tag.
3. Sample or template statements offered to instructors, not the institution's own rule. The fix is a new `binding` tag.
4. Compound sentences ("also covers P31"): each policy is already its own unit, so nothing to fix.

**Over-broad types the theme review must split, judging by how many fit notes point outside the type:** P24 (76 notes), P09 (61), P50 (45: data protection versus tool approval, now partly P92), P31 (34), P44 (30: prompting advice is now P93), P36 (30), P04 (30), P37 (27), P29 (27: ownership versus accuracy responsibility).
